The **Berlin-Warsaw Express** (technically a EuroCity service) is the most convenient way to travel between the two capitals. Since your conference is in **July 2026**, this is a fantastic "slow travel" alternative to flying, especially since it lands you right in the heart of Warsaw.

Here is the breakdown of what to expect:

### ⏱️ Schedule & Duration
* **Frequency:** Usually **6 to 7 direct trains per day**. 
* **Travel Time:** Approximately **5 hours and 30 minutes** to **6 hours**.
* **Key Stops:** * **Berlin Hauptbahnhof (Central Station):** Your likely starting point.
    * **Frankfurt (Oder):** The last German stop before crossing into Poland.
    * **Poznań Główny:** A major Polish city (great for a coffee break if you were stopping over).
    * **Warszawa Centralna:** Your main destination for the city center.

### 🍱 Onboard Experience
* **Dining Car (WARS):** This is often the highlight for travelers. The Polish **WARS** restaurant cars serve full, hot meals (like Pierogi or Żurek soup) and beer/coffee on real china. It is a much better experience than typical airline food!
* **Classes:** * **1st Class:** Offers more legroom, quieter carriages, and usually a free snack/drink.
    * **2nd Class:** Comfortable 6-person compartments or open-plan seating.
* **Connectivity:** Most trains are equipped with **free Wi-Fi** and **power outlets** at every seat, making it easy to get some conference prep done.

### ♿ Accessibility
The Berlin-Warsaw Express is well-equipped for wheelchair users, but it does require a little bit of pre-planning:
* **Dedicated Space:** Trains have a carriage with a dedicated wheelchair space and an accessible toilet.
* **Boarding Assistance:** You should book **Mobility Service** at least **24–48 hours in advance**. 
    * **In Germany:** Contact Deutsche Bahn’s *Mobility Service Centre*.
    * **In Poland:** Contact *PKP Intercity* assistance. 
    * Staff will meet you with a lift or ramp at the platform in Berlin and assist you off the train at *Warszawa Centralna*.

### 🎫 Booking Tips for 2026
* **Advance Tickets:** You can usually book up to **60 days in advance** on the Polish (PKP) site or **180 days** on the German (DB) site.
* **Mandatory Reservations:** Unlike some German trains, a **seat reservation is mandatory** for the Polish portion of this journey. It’s usually included in the price of your ticket.
* **Pricing:** Tickets start as low as **€30–€40** if booked early, but can jump to over €100 if bought on the day of travel.
